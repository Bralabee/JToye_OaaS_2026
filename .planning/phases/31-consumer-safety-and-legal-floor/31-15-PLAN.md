<objective>
Surface the order's allergen set on the kitchen display — an order-level banner that cannot be
missed and a per-item badge that says which dish — and carry it onto the printed ticket.

Purpose: D-04 requires both, and the reason is operational rather than aesthetic. A single aggregate
tells kitchen staff nothing actionable: they need to know the order contains sesame AND which of the
four dishes has it. Today the KDS item run is an inline comma-joined 12px `<span>` sequence
(`page.tsx:876-884`) carrying only quantity and name — it cannot carry a per-item badge legibly, which
is why the item list is restructured as part of this work.

The print sheet is not optional. `globals.css:181-325` renders a monochrome ticket, and
`kitchen-ticket.tsx:12-19` states the constraint: *"monochrome and unstyled by the brand — printers
are black-on-white and toner is not a design surface."* If the allergen block is not added to it, the
kitchen loses the warning at the exact moment it is acted on.

Output: two components, the restructured item list, and the printed block.
</objective>

THE VIEWING-DISTANCE CONTRACT, and it is why the sizes are what they are. The KDS is not read on a
phone at 30 cm. It assumes a wall-mounted or counter-propped display **0.6 m to 1.5 m** away, a
GLANCED read under time pressure, glare and steam, gloved fingers, and a reader who may be
colour-fatigued or colour-blind. At 20px a glyph subtends at 1.5 m what 8px does at 60 cm — legible
for a short uppercase label, not for a sentence. Hence: **short uppercase label plus short list,
never prose, in the banner.**

BANNER CONTRACT (UI-SPEC S4):
  · position: inside `CardHeader`, **directly under the order-number row**, above the customer name.
    Visible without scrolling the card.
  · treatment: **solid `bg-amber-800` fill with white text** (7.09:1). Solid, not a tint — a tint does
    not carry at 1.5 m. Full card width, `rounded-md`, `px-3 py-2`.
  · content: `AlertTriangle` icon + **"ALLERGENS"** at 20px/600 uppercase with tracking 0.08em
    (`text-xl font-semibold uppercase tracking-[0.08em]` — the base Heading size; **do not introduce
    an 18px fifth step**, which was blocked twice in UI-SPEC review) + the **complete** allergen list
    at 16px/600. **The banner NEVER truncates** — it is the place the full set is guaranteed to appear.
  · reconciliation line: a second line inside the banner prefixed **"CHECK:"**, naming the item and
    the undeclared allergen the ingredients text mentions. Distinct WORDING, not a distinct colour.
  · no-allergen orders: **render NOTHING.** A "no allergens" banner on every ticket trains staff to
    ignore the banner, which destroys the one thing it exists to do. Absence is the signal, and it is
    safe precisely because the banner is unmissable when present.
  · never colour-only: every allergen signal carries an icon AND words. The card's existing
    green/yellow/red age border (`page.tsx:88-93`) is **age**, not allergens — a second reason
    allergens use the amber family and a solid fill rather than a border.
  · motion: the banner does NOT animate, flash or pulse. A flashing safety warning is a seizure risk
    (WCAG 2.3.1) and an attention tax in a room that already has too many. The card's existing
    one-shot ember glow for new orders (`page.tsx:836-847`) is untouched.

PER-ITEM BADGE: on the item row after the name — amber-800 text on amber-50, `border-amber-700`,
14px/600. Shows up to **3** allergen names, then `+N`. Truncation is safe **only** because the banner

- **`textContent` concatenates**, so a `\bWord\b` match can fail after a digit, and a BUTTON labelled
  with a word satisfies a naive text search for that word. Scope text assertions to the banner
  element rather than the whole card.

  than reading the same tree twice.
- **`npm run build` is the type-check gate.** Jest does NOT type-check.

  is not caused by this plan. Run `scripts/seed-order-metric.sh` rather than investigating it.
- `grep -c` exits 1 on a zero count; always `out=$(grep -cF X f || true); grep -qx 0 <<< "$out"`.
