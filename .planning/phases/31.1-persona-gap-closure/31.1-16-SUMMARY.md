# Phase 31.1 Plan 16: Article 15 export, encrypted store and emailed link Summary

**A verified ACCESS request now assembles the subject's data across every tenant (tenant-pinned, NOSUPERUSER-proven), stores it AES-256-GCM encrypted behind a single-use SHA-256-hashed token (V75), emails the verified address a fragment link with no personal data, and completes only after both.**

## Accomplishments

- **#778 closed at the worker.** `DsarFanoutWorker` claims ACCESS as well as ERASURE. ACCESS collects in every tenant through the same per-tenant wrapper as erasure (`inPinnedTenant`: TenantContext.set, TransactionTemplate, GUC pin, asSystem, finally clear). It releases on any tenant failure, decrypt failure or lookup failure. Then it builds one document, stores and commits it, and sends the link. It writes COMPLETED (dropping the ciphertext) only when the mailer returns true. The backlog WARN `dsar_access_requests_outstanding` and `outstandingAccessRequests()` are gone.
- **V75 `dsar_access_export`** holds only the token's SHA-256, an encrypted payload and an expiry. It has a single-use CHECK, one row per request (a retry replaces the row) and ON DELETE CASCADE. The V62-style header explains why it is not tenant-scoped and what protects it instead. It is exempted by addition in `RlsContractTest`, and the exemption is proven load-bearing.
- **The export document (`jtoye-dsar-export/1`)** contains: summary (`vendorDataHeld`, statement), about (purposes, recipients, retention pointer, rights incl. ICO, source), platformAccount, and one section per vendor. The vendor section carries the recipient (legal name, entity type, company/VAT number, address, shops), customer records, orders (address, notes, allergy note, when the shop read it, acknowledged allergens with null meaning not recorded, totals, items), reviews (with photos), communication preferences and staff directory. Per tenant it is a superset of the Article 20 export.
- **The email** is plain text: `{export-download-base-url}#token=<43 base64url>`. It says the link works once and expires in 7 days, that the file lists each shop, and gives the privacy-notice link. It contains no order, name, item or vendor (test-tier prohibition, proven able to fail).
- **Deterministic:** vendors are sorted by legal name (nulls last) then tenant id, records by time then id, and all timestamps are UTC. Two exports of the same data are byte-identical apart from `generatedAt`.

## Deviations from Plan

### Auto-fixed Issues

**1. [Rule 1 - Bug] Claimed DSAR requests ran in an arbitrary order**
- **Found during:** Task 2 RED. The ACCESS+ERASURE arm went red because the erasure ran first and the access export said `"vendors":[]`.
- **Issue:** `CLAIM_SQL` orders its subquery by `received_at`, but `UPDATE ... RETURNING` returns rows in no guaranteed order. A subject who asked for a copy and then for erasure could receive "we hold nothing". This also contradicted the yml comment "a backlog drains in the order it was lodged".
- **Fix:** RETURNING now includes `received_at`, and the worker sorts the claimed rows by (received_at, id) before executing them.
- **Files modified:** DsarFanoutWorker.java
- **Verification:** the arm is green with the fix. Without it the arm went red on 1 of 2 runs (T2-3, non-deterministic by nature).
- **Committed in:** `42c9f0d0`

**2. [Rule 3 - Blocking] k8s env contract broken by the new placeholder**
