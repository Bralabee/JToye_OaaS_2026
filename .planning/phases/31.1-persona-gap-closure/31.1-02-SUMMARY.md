## Observation: the record hash means two different things

`eraseCustomerData` writes `subject_email_sha256 = sha256Hex(raw customers.email)`, with no trim and no lower-case. That was true before this plan and still is. Guest records written by the fan-out carry `DsarSubjectDigest.of(...)`, which is trimmed and `Locale.ROOT` lower-cased. Two consequences follow:
- A customer stored as `Jane@X.com` produces a record whose hash is not the DSAR digest of that address.
- An operator who wants to answer "did we erase this person?" must hash the address raw for a record that names a customer, and normalised for a record whose `subject_customer_id` is NULL.

The plan forbids changing the meaning of historic rows, so this was not changed. Unifying the two needs either a dual-hash window or a recorded cut-over date.

## Decisions Made

See `key-decisions` in the frontmatter. The material one: a customers-row subject in a tenant gets exactly one record, because the first matched customer's erasure also sweeps the subject's other stored spellings. The plan's literal sequence (erase the customer, then run a guest pass over "the remaining variants") would write a second record whenever a guest order carried a different spelling. That breaks the must-have "each tenant that held the subject gets exactly one record".
