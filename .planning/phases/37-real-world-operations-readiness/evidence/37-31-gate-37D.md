### 1.6 OPEN product finding for the owner (recorded here, NOT fixed in this plan)

**A signed-in customer's storefront order shares one IP digest with every other signed-in customer at that shop.**
37-30 found it; this gate records it as a decision the owner has to take before a shared environment accepts
signed-in cash orders. The access token is an HttpOnly cookie, so a signed-in order is posted through the Next
route `POST /api/customer-orders?shop=` which adds `X-Customer-Token` server-side; core's socket peer is then the
FRONTEND CONTAINER, and `ClientIpResolver` (37-24, D-19) reads `X-Forwarded-For` only from a peer in
`jtoye.security.trusted-proxies` (`TRUSTED_PROXY_CIDRS`, empty by default). So every signed-in cash order at a
shop is digested under the frontend's address, and the per-IP cap (5 open, 37-28) can refuse a sixth legitimate
signed-in customer while five others have open cash orders there. Guest orders are unaffected (the browser posts
straight to core, so the peer is the client).

**Recommended fix (research-then-recommend ruling 2026-10-10; deferred-items carries the same):** in
`CashOrderCaps`, skip the IP axis for an order whose contact is VERIFIED — the IP axis exists for anonymous floods
and a verified contact is already bounded by the email axis — which also removes the dependency on proxy topology.
The operator alternative is declaring the frontend's range in `TRUSTED_PROXY_CIDRS` per environment behind an
ingress that sets `X-Forwarded-For` (the route forwards the chain it receives). A one-line owner decision; it is
why `abuse-bounds.spec.ts` places its six-order IP proof as a GUEST and uses a vendor-placed (NULL) order, not a
proxy-placed one, as the badge's negative control.
