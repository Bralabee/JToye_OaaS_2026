<every_render_branch>
**TIER EVERY PAGE-LEVEL RENDER BRANCH, NOT JUST THE LOADED ONE.** This is the correction that matters
most in this plan, and it was nearly shipped as a defect.

These pages are not single-return components. Each has early returns for loading and error states, and
an instruction to edit "the root element of the component's main return" reaches exactly one of them.
The consequence is concrete: after this plan the Shell band is ~1700 wide, so an untiered error branch
would render its content at the FULL shell width while the loaded branch renders at 1100 — the page
would jump roughly 600px the moment the fetch resolves or fails. That is the SAME defect class plan
35-07 is fixing on `/shop/[slug]` (a skeleton 384px wider than its content), except newly INTRODUCED
by this phase rather than inherited. A width contract that creates a width jump has failed on its own
terms.

THE BRANCH MAP, read off the tree rather than assumed. Line numbers will drift; the SHAPE is the
contract, so locate each by its guard condition, not by its number.

<displaced_goods>
**THIS PLAN NARROWS THREE LIVE USER-VISIBLE SURFACES, AND THE DOCTRINE REQUIRES THAT BE ENUMERATED.**

The Incremental Betterment doctrine is explicit: *"Any plan that reworks an existing user-visible
surface MUST enumerate the goods it displaces and account for each one."* An earlier draft of this plan
enumerated only the surfaces it does NOT touch, and framed the risk one-directionally in T-35-16 —
"would ship 300px wider". Both directions are real, and the narrowing direction is the one that
actually ships. Plan 35-06 does this properly and is the model.

THE ARITHMETIC, and it is arithmetic rather than an estimate. `main` = viewport − 256px sidebar. The
shell band's CONTENT box = its width − 64px, because it carries `p-4 sm:p-8` (32px each side at every
viewport at or above `sm`). Pre-change the band is the stock shadcn `container`, capped 1400.

| Viewport | Order detail TODAY | After this plan | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1440 | min(1184, 1400) − 64 = **1120** | 1100 | **−20** |
| 1920 | min(1664, 1400) − 64 = **1336** | 1100 | **−236** |
| 2560 | min(2304, 1400) − 64 = **1336** | 1100 | **−236** |

The same three numbers apply to `/dashboard/onboarding` and `/dashboard/products/import`, which
inherit the identical band. So at 1920 and above, this plan takes 236px AWAY from three surfaces that
have it today. That is a displaced good and it is not incidental — it is most of what this plan does.

**THE JUSTIFICATION, which has to carry that 236px.** These are reading and form surfaces, not data
grids. CONTEXT.md section 3's third independent finding is that detail and reading columns cluster
tightly at **1016–1136px** across the closest measurable peers — Linear's detail ladder tops out at
1136, Square's at 1016, Lightspeed's content column is 1100 — and prose-measure guidance (45–75
characters per line) is why. A 1336px order detail is already past that cluster; 1100 sits inside it,
level with Lightspeed and 36px under Linear. The order detail page is a labelled key–value read plus a
line-item list; the onboarding page is a sequential gate form; the import wizard is a tabbed form. None
gains from width, and long measures measurably cost a reader line-tracking accuracy.

So the narrowing is the INTENDED product change, not a side effect of the tier: this is the half of
the phase that protects against its own enthusiasm. CONTEXT section 3 states it directly — *"widening a
settings form or an order detail to 1700px would be a regression, not an improvement"* — and without
this plan these three surfaces would inherit the 1700 Shell band and land at ~1636, which is 300px
WIDER than today and further from the peer cluster in the wrong direction. The choice is not
"1336 or 1100"; it is "1636 or 1100", and 1100 is the defensible end of that range.

**THE −20 AT 1440 IS THE ONE TO WATCH**, because 1440 is the laptop width where the owner was told
nothing would move. It is small, it is real, and it is surfaced explicitly at the 35-13 gate rather
than left for the owner to trip over. Do not describe 1440 as unchanged anywhere in this plan's
outputs.

**IF THE OWNER REJECTS THE NARROWING AT THE GATE**, the remedy is to revise DETAIL_MAX_PX in
`frontend/lib/layout-widths.ts` — one number, one place, which is exactly why plan 35-01 declared it
there. Do not respond by untiering the surfaces, which would silently hand them the 1636 Shell band.

Goods NOT displaced, and confirmed preserved: every page's existing vertical rhythm and padding
classes; the centring, which is newly added so the narrower band sits mid-band rather than hugging the
left edge; and every surface in the ceiling block below.
</displaced_goods>

<tier_is_a_ceiling>
A CONTRACT RULE this plan establishes, and plan 35-11 writes into the contract document:

**A tier is a ceiling, not a target.** A surface that is already narrower than its tier for a stated
reason keeps its measure and is recorded as an exception. Widening a 672px Stripe Connect status
panel of three paragraphs to 1100px because "it is a Detail surface" would be a regression, and the
Incremental Betterment doctrine names that class of change directly: it trades away a working good
to satisfy a rule.

The exceptions in the dashboard, all verified present in the tree and all to be LEFT ALONE:
  - the Stripe Connect return and refresh outcome panel, shared by both routes, at 672px
  - every Radix dialog width (portal-rendered, outside the page container, so the page tier cannot
    reach them and their width is a modal-ergonomics decision, not a layout-contract one)
  - the truncation clamps on table cells and empty-state copy measures

AND THE PUBLIC/STOREFRONT SET, which PATTERNS enumerates and which this plan must RECORD even though
nothing ships wrong on any of them. CONTEXT section 5 and the standing N/A rule both require a
deliberate non-change to be written down: an unstated boundary reads as covered, and "we examined this
and left it" is indistinguishable from "we never looked" unless someone writes it. Every one is already
NARROWER than the Detail tier for a stated ergonomic reason, so each is an instance of this same
ceiling-not-target rule reached from the public side:

  - `app/shop/[slug]/cart/page.tsx`                    `max-w-2xl` ×2 (672)  — a linear checkout step
  - `app/shop/[slug]/checkout/page.tsx`                 `max-w-2xl` ×4 (672)  — address, payment, confirm
  - `app/shop/[slug]/orders/[orderNumber]/page.tsx`     `max-w-lg`  ×4 (512)  — a customer receipt
  - `app/shop/orders/orders-client.tsx`                 `max-w-lg`  ×3 (512)  — a phone-first history list
  - `app/track/page.tsx`                                `max-w-lg`     (512)  — a two-field lookup form
  - `app/unsubscribe/unsubscribe-content.tsx`           `max-w-lg`     (512)  — a one-click opt-out panel

Widening any of them would strand a single-purpose transactional panel across a desktop screen. They
are NOT edited by this plan and NOT given a tier attribute — an attribute would claim a tier the
surface has not been assigned. Record them in the exceptions ledger; plan 35-10 carries them into the
manifest so the record is machine-readable rather than prose only.
</tier_is_a_ceiling>
