    - The convergent-cluster finding: three independent products sit within 40px of each other in the
      1680 to 1720 band for an application shell, which is 88% of a 1920 screen and about two thirds of
      2560 — landing on the owner's original instinct rather than merely near it.
    - The Index tier's rule: resource indexes with many columns take no cap below the shell, with the
      Polaris, Carbon, GitLab and Lightspeed precedent named. Include the delivery-log exception (a
      route that looks like a detail route and is tiered Index because of what it holds), or the next
      reader will correct it back.
    - THE CEILING RULE, established by plan 35-05: a tier is a ceiling, not a target. A surface already
      narrower for a stated reason keeps its measure. This is the rule that stops the contract being
      applied mechanically into a regression.
    - The exceptions, enumerated with their reasons: the Stripe Connect status panel; portal-rendered
      dialogs, whose width is a modal-ergonomics decision the page tier cannot reach anyway; truncation
      clamps and typographic measures; the public shop directory staying at the Marketing width rather
      than going fluid, with its three reasons; the shop detail menu keeping its current measure with
      the widening deferred; the policy pages' outer band and the legal index left alone, both recorded
      as explicit N/A rather than dropped.
    - The PUBLIC AND STOREFRONT deliberate non-changes, listed by surface with their measures and their
      reasons, from plan 35-05's exceptions ledger: the basket review and checkout steps at 672, the
      customer receipt and order history at 512, the guest tracking form and the unsubscribe panel at
      512. Nothing ships wrong on any of them, which is exactly why they are easy to omit — and an
      omission here reads as "never examined". Each is an instance of the ceiling rule reached from the
      public side: already narrower for a stated ergonomic reason, so it keeps its measure.
    - The two N/A-with-a-trap surfaces: `components/public/public-shell.tsx`, whose `<main>` is
      deliberately width-free and where a cap must NOT be added (`/shop` is a separate layout tree,
      `PolicyPage` nests inside, and a 375px no-overflow assertion depends on it); and
      `app/shop/[slug]/not-found.tsx`, the third member of the `/shop/[slug]` 896px width family
      alongside the loading skeleton and the detail client.
    - The DISPLACED-GOODS record for the Detail tier, from plan 35-05: this contract makes order
      detail, onboarding and the import wizard about 236px NARROWER at 1920 and 2560 and about 20px
      narrower at 1440. State it plainly with the peer-cluster justification. A width standard that
      documents only its widenings is not a standard, and this is the one number a future reader is
      most likely to challenge.
    - The two application shapes — in place on an existing band, wrapper only where no band exists —
      and why in-place is preferred on animated surfaces.
    - Where the numbers live, where the class literals live, and why those are different directories:
      the build's content globs do not cover the library directory, so a class string there is silently
      never generated. This is the single most expensive thing for a newcomer to rediscover.
    - The coverage boundary, stated plainly and WITHOUT overstating it. Which tiers are asserted on a
      pull request (Marketing only) and which are not (Shell, Index, Detail). For the latter, do NOT
      write "covered nightly": issue **#683** is open, the full-suite nightly lane is dark, and it is
      the project's only full-suite E2E instrument. The honest sentence, and CONTEXT.md section 5
      carries it as authoritative, is that those tiers are **"covered by a spec that no current tree
      executes"**, with #683 named so a reader can check whether it has since been fixed. A document
      that claims an instrument is watching a tier is worse than one that admits none is — the first
      stops anyone looking. Note the same root cause for **#686**, whose skip-budget gate is wired only
      into that same dark lane. Also state that the per-PR accessibility gate cannot evaluate the
      scroll-region rule at all, because that rule is runtime-geometric and jsdom reports zero for both
      dimensions it needs.
    - A pointer to the manifest and the gate, so a reader knows the document is enforced and where.

    Two constraints on the writing. Do not quote any test count or suite figure — those are gated
    against the metrics manifest and would make this document a maintenance liability for no benefit.

    the documents in its set, and a citation that drifts is worse than none, because a reader follows
    it, sees something unrelated, and concludes the document is right and their understanding is wrong.
    Prefer naming files without line numbers.

    Add the document to `docs/DOCUMENTATION_INDEX.md` if that index enumerates architecture documents.
