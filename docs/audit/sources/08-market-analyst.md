# UK Food-SaaS Market Analysis (2025-2026)

**Analyst persona**: UK hospitality tech industry analyst
**Date**: 2026-04-27
**Sources**: 24 verified URLs cited inline; ~6 unverified claims (Slerp, Otter, OrderYOYO, Deliverect, Storekit, Zonal pricing) explicitly marked due to gated/quote-based pricing pages.

---

## Executive summary

The UK food-vendor SaaS market in 2025-26 is **a crowded, well-capitalised, consolidating field** with no single dominant player. Toast (US) is aggressively buying market share, ending 2025 with ~164,000 global locations and ~2,000+ in the UK/IE/CA region (verified, [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/power-over-160-000-restaurants-114834489.html)). Square holds the low end with a free POS and £69/site Plus tier (verified, [squareup.com](https://squareup.com/help/gb/en/article/6415-square-for-restaurants-pricing)). The "stack-buyer" pattern dominates: **Access Group** acquired Paytronix, QikServe and SHR in 2024; **Shift4** rolled up Revel, Vectron and Givex (verified, [Hospitality Tech](https://hospitalitytech.com/restaurant-technology-ma-ripe-consolidation-2025-2026)); **Prosus** took Just Eat Takeaway for €4.1bn in 2025 ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/business/2025/08/11/eu-clears-41bn-just-eat-takeover-but-prevents-food-delivery-mega-merger)). Three trends define 2026: (1) **commission-free direct ordering** as a positioning wedge against 25-35% aggregator take rates; (2) **AI as utility infrastructure** (menu engineering, voice ordering); (3) **vertical "operating system" bundling** — POS + KDS + online ordering + marketing in one SKU. A new entrant's realistic wedges: **price** (sub-£50/site/month all-in), **UK-native compliance** (Natasha's Law + MTD baked-in), and **multi-tenant white-label** for the long tail of UK independents.

## Deep profiles

### Toast
US-origin (founded 2011, Boston), publicly traded on NYSE as TOST. Toast has scaled from ~140,000 locations at start of 2025 to ~164,000 by year end (verified, [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/power-over-160-000-restaurants-114834489.html)), with explicit international expansion into UK, Ireland, Canada, and Australia. UK pricing reflects a tiered SaaS model: Starter £80/mo, Essential £150/mo, with in-person card processing 2.49% + £0.15 and £799+ hardware per terminal (verified, [Jelly UK guide](https://blog.getjelly.co.uk/toast-integration-costs-2026/)). The full stack covers POS, KDS, online ordering, loyalty, payroll, and inventory; multi-site real-world spend exceeds £1,800/mo. Toast's competitive moat is its data network (160k+ locations feeding analytics) and vertical integration of payments (high-margin take rate). Risk to UK challengers: Toast can subsidise UK acquisition with US payment-processing margins.

### Square for Restaurants
US-origin (Block, Inc.). UK pricing is the most transparent in the market: Free, Plus at £69+VAT/site/mo (with unlimited KDS devices), Premium custom for >£200k annual processing (verified, [squareup.com](https://squareup.com/help/gb/en/article/6415-square-for-restaurants-pricing)). Card processing 1.75% in-person, 1.4% + 25p online — materially cheaper than Toast on card. Hardware: Square Stand £99, Terminal £149, Register £699, KDS screen £549. Square's strength is **lowest barrier to entry** for UK independents: a single-site cafe can be live for under £200 hardware + £69/mo. Weakness: thin enterprise/multi-site features, weak delivery integrations. Square is the de-facto baseline a new entrant must beat on either price or feature depth.

### Deliverect
Belgian unicorn ($1.9B valuation, $332M raised, $44.7M revenue Oct-2024 per [Crunchbase / Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/deliverect)). Acquired Tabesto in Dec 2024 to add kiosk capability ([Tracxn funding](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/deliverect/__jXSim2jUttzz1gdYuc0GIpCr02uXraxqPJ6c9rDANF4/funding-and-investors)). Deliverect is **not a competitor to a full POS** — it's the connective tissue between aggregators (Deliveroo/UE/JE) and POS/KDS. UK pricing is gated behind a sales call ([deliverect.com/en-gb/pricing](https://www.deliverect.com/en-gb/pricing)); legacy public tiers were €69-€166/mo (unverified for 2026). Strength: 99.8% successful order injection rate, used by enterprise QSR. Strategic threat: if Deliverect builds its own POS or partners with a low-cost POS, it could disintermediate the J'Toye-style stack from above.

### Flipdish
Irish-founded, UK-strong with independents and chains. $157M raised (mostly Series C 2022 led by Tencent/Tiger), ~$20M ARR end-2024, no major 2024-2025 funding round announced ([Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/flipdish/__hHnNN8w6rUI322VRiMJU7fj5up_RFeZUAfUs3vWeFwg)). Verified UK pricing: Essential bundle £119/mo annual or £139/mo monthly ([Flipdish UK pricing page](https://www.flipdish.com/gb/pricing)) — bundles POS + branded site/app + caller ID + CRM + payroll. The 7%+VAT per-order commission cited in older sources is **unverified for 2026**; the bundle pricing suggests this may have shifted to subscription-heavy. Flipdish's branded mobile app is its main differentiator versus Square. Strength: white-label app is hard to replicate cheaply. Weakness: per-order fees compound at scale, eroding the "commission-free" pitch.

### Vita Mojo
UK-native, founded 2015, the strongest mid-to-large UK QSR and casual dining player. Closed $30M led by Battery Ventures in Jan 2025 (verified, [Vita Mojo blog](https://www.vitamojo.com/blog/big-news-announcing-our-latest-investment-round/)), bringing total to $59.8M. Pricing is modular and quote-driven; SaaSWorthy quotes "from £50/mo" but enterprise deals are materially higher. Vita Mojo's positioning is the **"Restaurant OS for chains"** — kiosks, KDS, mobile ordering, loyalty, and analytics in one stack. Customers include Pho, LEON, Nando's (per industry coverage). Direct competitive overlap with J'Toye is high on the multi-channel order/KDS layer, but Vita Mojo is enterprise-focused with bespoke onboarding — leaves a long tail of UK independents under-served.

### Slerp
UK-native, boutique-food-brand specialist. Modest scale: ~$4.4M ARR Sep-2025, ~40 staff ([getlatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/slerp.com)). Pricing not publicly listed; positions on **0% commission on direct orders** versus Deliveroo's ~25%. Strength: brand quality (works with high-end indie food brands like Pasta Evangelists historically), strong CRM and loyalty story. Weakness: limited POS/KDS depth — Slerp is a **front-end ordering layer**, not a full operating system. If J'Toye nails the back-of-house (KDS, allergens, payments) it can co-exist with or replace Slerp for vendors needing kitchen-fulfilment depth.

## KDS market sub-segment

KDS has shifted from a hardware-heavy capex item to a tablet-app SaaS line. Three buying patterns:

| KDS option | Pricing | Hardware | Posture |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Toast KDS** | Bundled with Toast SaaS (£150+/mo); £799+ heat-rated terminal | Proprietary, heat-tempered | Vertical-integrated, premium |
| **Square KDS** | Included with Plus (£69/mo); £549 21.5" screen | Off-the-shelf Android display | Best price on bundled KDS |
| **Fresh KDS** | $15/mo per screen | Standard iOS/Android tablet | Best-in-class standalone, integrates with many POS |
| **Deliverect (kitchen relay)** | Bundled with order management | Tablet display | Aggregator-focused, not a full KDS |
| **Vita Mojo KDS** | Quote-driven enterprise | Tablet | Tied to Vita Mojo POS |
| **J'Toye (claimed)** | TBD | Web/STOMP/WebSocket via browser | Standalone, no proprietary HW |

KDS as a standalone SKU caps out around **$15-30/mo per screen** (Fresh KDS, KDSCom). Verified at [Merchant Maverick](https://www.merchantmaverick.com/kitchen-display-system/) and [Loman](https://loman.ai/blog/best-kitchen-display-systems-order-routing). J'Toye's WebSocket/STOMP architecture is technically equivalent to incumbents — the moat for KDS is **integration breadth** (which POS/aggregators connect), not screen rendering.

## UK regulatory landscape

- **Natasha's Law** (PPDS labelling, in force since Oct 2021): Pre-Packed for Direct Sale items must carry an ingredient list with the 14 statutory allergens emphasised. The FSA expanded guidance in **March 2025** encouraging written allergen info for non-PPDS too (verified, [Infinitas](https://www.infinitasfoodsafety.com/natashas-law-food-allergen-labelling-caterers-retailers-guide-2025/), [Foodsconnected](https://blog.foodsconnected.com/natashas-law-changing-labelling-legislation)). A SaaS that auto-generates compliant labels (J'Toye claims OpenPDF allergen labels) addresses a real and growing pain.
- **Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT**: Mandatory for all VAT-registered businesses (threshold £90k as of Aug-2025). Restaurants must keep digital records and submit returns via MTD-compatible software (verified, [The Hospitality Accountants](https://thehospitalityaccountants.com/tax/what-making-tax-digital-for-hospitality-businesses-really-means/), [Taxoo](https://www.taxoo.co.uk/vat/)). HMRC compliance checks rose throughout 2025. POS platforms typically integrate with Xero/QuickBooks/Sage for MTD; native MTD bridge is a defensible feature for a UK-native SaaS.
- **PCI DSS**: Standard for any card processor — Stripe handles PCI scope offload, which J'Toye's Stripe integration already inherits.
- **Consumer Duty (FCA)**: Only relevant if J'Toye ever introduces consumer credit (e.g., BNPL like Klarna). Currently out of scope but a risk if "pay later" features are added.

## Trends 2025-2026

1. **AI as utility, not novelty.** Industry sources frame 2026 as "the year AI moved from experiment to infrastructure" ([QSR Web](https://www.qsrweb.com/articles/why-2026-is-the-year-of-the-ai-driven-restaurant/)). Top use cases: marketing/personalisation (53%), predictive analytics (40%), voice ordering (39%) per [Toast research](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/ai-restaurant-data). J'Toye's Ollama LLM image-analysis is on-trend but undifferentiated unless tied to menu engineering or food-safety detection.
2. **Voice ordering goes mainstream.** Voice AI is being marketed as utility-grade in 2026 (verified trend, [Menutiger](https://www.menutiger.com/blog/restaurant-technology-trends)). Phone-call ordering for takeaways is the obvious wedge for UK independents.
3. **Aggregator backlash continues.** Deliveroo (~25%, capped 30%), Uber Eats (~30-35%), Just Eat (14% + 50p admin + £699 listing fee) commissions remain economic poison for thin-margin operators ([MerchantSwitch](https://www.merchantswitch.com/blog/delivery-platform-comparison-uk)). Direct ordering platforms (Slerp, Storekit, Flipdish, OrderYOYO) all wedge here. Customer-side: Deliveroo orders cost 31% more than direct, Uber Eats 25%, Just Eat 7% ([Which.co.uk](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/the-hidden-costs-of-the-food-delivery-revolution-the-surprising-premium-added-to-your-next-takeaway-a9kAZ8K7lB30)).
4. **Stack consolidation by acquisition.** Access Group (Paytronix, QikServe, SHR), Shift4 (Revel, Vectron, Givex), Prosus (Just Eat Takeaway €4.1bn) all in 2024-25 ([Hospitality Tech](https://hospitalitytech.com/restaurant-technology-ma-ripe-consolidation-2025-2026), [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/business/2025/08/11/eu-clears-41bn-just-eat-takeover-but-prevents-food-delivery-mega-merger)). Two more years of M&A are forecast.
5. **Unified digital ecosystem.** "Connected setup" — POS + online ordering + KDS + loyalty in one back-end — is the table-stakes 2026 architecture (verified industry consensus, [MobiDev](https://mobidev.biz/blog/restaurant-technology-trends-innovations-implementation)).
6. **Dark kitchens commoditised.** Boom of 2020-22 has stabilised; the SaaS opportunity has shifted from "dark kitchen ops" to **menu-aggregation/multi-brand-per-location** tooling (where Deliverect dominates).

## Where a UK-native vertical SaaS could wedge in 2026

There are **three credible openings** for a new UK entrant:

1. **Sub-£50/site/mo, no per-order commission, all-in.** Square is £69 + payment processing; Flipdish is £119+; Toast is £80-£150 + processing + hardware. The independent UK takeaway/cafe segment is hugely price-sensitive. A genuinely all-inclusive £39-49/mo SKU (POS + ordering + KDS + Stripe pass-through + branded subdomain + Natasha's Law labels) with **no per-order commission** would slot below Square's effective cost-of-ownership for a busy independent. The hard part: card processing economics — without Square/Toast's payment-processing margin, sustainability requires Stripe Connect platform fees in the 0.5-1% range.

2. **Compliance-first positioning for UK independents.** Natasha's Law (Oct-2021) plus the March-2025 FSA expansion to non-PPDS, plus MTD VAT, plus PPDS allergen labelling are real friction points. None of the global incumbents (Toast, Square) make compliance a headline feature. A UK-native platform can ship **MTD bridge + Natasha's Law label printer + allergen-aware menus** as a single value proposition — and charge for the certainty rather than the features.

3. **Multi-tenant white-label for franchise/multi-brand operators.** Most incumbent stacks are single-tenant. J'Toye's multi-tenant RLS architecture is well-suited to **dark-kitchen operators, franchise networks, and ghost-brand portfolios** that need to spin up new "shops" in minutes. This is the architectural advantage that maps cleanest to a unique commercial wedge — but it requires explicit packaging as "operate-as-a-service" rather than "POS SaaS" to differentiate.

The realistic strategy is to **pick exactly one** of these wedges and ship it deeply. Trying all three dilutes the message against well-funded incumbents.

## What J'Toye is missing vs table stakes

Based on CLAUDE.md feature claims compared to verified incumbent feature pages:

- **Branded native mobile app** (iOS + Android) — Flipdish, Toast and OrderYOYO all ship this; storefront alone is insufficient for the takeaway segment.
- **Aggregator integration** (Deliveroo, Uber Eats, Just Eat menu sync) — even commission-free competitors (Slerp, Storekit) integrate aggregators because vendors won't drop them overnight. Otherwise J'Toye forces an "either/or" choice that vendors will reject. Most do this via Deliverect, which is the de-facto integration layer.
- **Loyalty + automated marketing campaigns** — J'Toye claims "marketing tools"; needs to be specific (SMS, email automation, loyalty points, win-back campaigns) to match Flipdish/Toast.
- **Reservations / table management** — Access Collins (£135/mo), OpenTable, Resy. If J'Toye targets dine-in alongside takeaway, reservations are missing.
- **MTD VAT bridge / accounting integration** (Xero, QuickBooks, Sage) — table-stakes for any UK-resident SaaS handling money.
- **Inventory and stock management with COGS** — Vita Mojo, Toast, Lightspeed all ship this; missing in J'Toye's claimed feature list.
- **Payroll / staff scheduling** — Flipdish and Toast bundle this; might be out of scope but is a typical bundle expectation.
- **Public pricing page** — every credible competitor (except quote-driven Deliverect/Vita Mojo/Zonal) publishes pricing. Hidden pricing is a trust killer for the SMB segment.
- **G2 / Capterra presence** — incumbents have hundreds of reviews; a new entrant needs review-platform presence as social proof.
- **PCI / compliance certification page** — table-stakes trust signal.

## Competitive risk: who would copy J'Toye fastest if it gained traction?

In rough order of likelihood and speed-to-copy:

1. **Square** — could ship multi-tenant + Natasha's Law support in a single quarter. Square already has the cheapest distribution and has historically responded to UK regulatory features (e.g., contactless tap-to-pay) within 6 months. **Highest threat**.
2. **Flipdish** — already UK/IE-rooted, has white-label app and brand, $157M war-chest. Could close any feature gap by acquisition rather than build (their playbook to date — Jinoby acquired May 2023). **High threat**.
3. **Toast** — would treat J'Toye as a sub-scale UK indie threat, but has the financial firepower to ship anything. Toast's UK push is the biggest macro risk to *all* UK SaaS players, J'Toye included. **High threat, but slow-moving on feature parity.**
4. **Deliverect** — could build downward into POS/KDS rather than just middleware; their Tabesto acquisition (kiosk) signals appetite. If Deliverect packages Tabesto + a basic POS, J'Toye's middle layer is squeezed. **Medium threat.**
5. **Vita Mojo** — well-funded ($59.8M total) and UK-native, but enterprise-focused. Unlikely to chase J'Toye downmarket; more likely to ignore the long tail. **Low-medium threat.**
6. **Access Group** — would acquire rather than build. If J'Toye gains material traction, an Access Group / Shift4 / Lightspeed acquisition offer is a plausible exit, not a competitive threat. **Strategic threat = exit opportunity.**
7. **A new YC/Seedcamp entrant** — the 2024-25 funding climate has produced multiple "Toast for X" startups. The next obvious one is a sub-£50 UK-native takeaway SaaS, which is exactly the J'Toye target. **Medium-high threat from peers, not incumbents.**

The single greatest commercial risk is not a feature copy — it is **Toast and Square continuing to invest in UK acquisition** while bundling payments at near-cost. A new UK entrant cannot win a payments-margin price war and must wedge on compliance, multi-tenancy, or vertical specificity rather than core POS features.
